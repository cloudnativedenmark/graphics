## Your role

Thank you for being a track host. It means a lot to the organisers who keep everything running, and even more to the speakers, who will now have a friendly face welcoming them and cheering for them.

Your job is to be the point of contact for speakers during the day. Take care of the small practicalities, and make the adjustments to the introduction and running of each talk that make all the difference to the speaker and the audience.

This is a new concept for us, so don't hesitate to reach out to us or your fellow hosts with ideas and suggestions for making it better.

## Before the talk (T–10)

- Be at the stage, make sure the speaker has arrived, and help them set up. If they're not there, or there's any trouble, find an organiser or call one of the [organisers](#organisers).
- Welcome the speaker to the stage and have a brief touch-base.
- Get in sync on how the talk will go. Understand what they're planning: a live Q&A, a crowd exercise, or a regular talk. Help them during the talk if asked.
- Mention how much time they have. There will be a timer visible to the speaker, but offer to give them a signal as they near the end, if they'd like one.
- Make sure they have water at hand for the talk.
- Ask if there's anything they need, and help provide it. Be their support, and let them feel welcome and taken care of.
- Agree with the speaker how you will introduce them. They might already have plans.

## During the talk

- Kick off the talk by welcoming the speaker to the stage as agreed. A simple "Please welcome to the stage *Name*, *Job title*" and a round of applause can be enough.
- Enjoy the show.
- Be mindful of the schedule shifting. If the talk itself ran long, there may not be time for Q&A, even if you agreed to it. If that happens, give the signal if you agreed on one, and suggest attendees take the discussion to the hallway.

## Ending the talk

- It's usually best to let the speaker end the session, rather than closing it for them.
- For Q&A, join the speaker on stage and read them selected questions from Slido. Mind the time: you don't need to read all of them.
- If applicable, moderate Slido questions for harmful or inappropriate content.
- If the session is particularly good, or there are lots of questions, help the speaker moderate. It may make sense to let the session run a little over time. Use your best judgement.
- If there are more questions than time, suggest taking the discussion to the hallway.
- When a talk closes, people typically start leaving the room immediately. If you have something important to communicate, such as logistics, confidently take over after the talk and clearly state the call to action.
